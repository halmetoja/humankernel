# Human Kernel / Theory of Psychological Autonomy

## Canonical master context for AI models

This document is the self-contained working context for the **Human Kernel**, **Theory of Psychological Autonomy**, and current **SRF** project. It should be sufficient for an AI model, researcher, editor, critic, or collaborator who has no access to the conversations from which the theory developed.

The project is owned and authored by **Jani Halmetoja**. AI models may help organize, test, criticize, formalize, simulate, research, and write the theory, but they must not silently claim its original hypotheses as established psychology.

The book's current working title is:

> **Human Kernel**

Current Finnish subtitle:

> **Psykologisen autonomian arkkitehtuuri**

The title is intentionally short, comprehensible, ambitious, and slightly audacious. *Kernel* is an engineering metaphor, not a proposed anatomical structure.

## 1. How to use this context

### 1.1 Epistemic labels

Use the following labels whenever theoretical status matters:

- **[ESTABLISHED]** — a broad phenomenon or research tradition is well supported, although the summary may simplify it.
- **[BRIDGE]** — adjacent evidence makes the connection plausible, but does not establish the Human Kernel mechanism.
- **[HYPOTHESIS]** — a central, currently unvalidated claim specific to Human Kernel/SRF.
- **[EXTENSION]** — a speculative application or less-developed consequence.
- **[OPERATIONAL]** — a proposed variable, metric, equation, task, or simulation design; not yet validated.
- **[METAPHOR]** — an explanatory engineering analogy, not a literal claim about the brain.
- **[OPEN]** — a question deliberately left unresolved.

Do not silently upgrade a hypothesis into a finding. Do not use phrases such as “Human Kernel proves” or “the brain's kernel” unless explicitly discussing the metaphor.

### 1.2 Language rules

Preserve the project's canonical terms:

- Human Kernel
- psychological autonomy
- representation and representational state
- differentiation
- simultaneity
- integration
- psychological wholeness
- process isolation
- structural centrality
- keystone representation
- conditions of possibility
- structural consequences
- representational resolution
- salience
- meaning assignment
- self-relevance weighting
- compression
- defensive reconstruction
- internal regulation
- external regulation
- developmental bifurcation
- self-sacrifice
- other-sacrifice
- Blame–Responsibility Market
- Blame–Responsibility Routing Network

Prefer plain psychological language in the book. Engineering terminology should clarify causal structure, dependencies, state transitions, or failure modes. If a technical metaphor merely renames an existing psychological word, remove it.

Do not make internal references such as “Paper 9” in book prose. The reader is not expected to know the SRF paper sequence.

### 1.3 Diagnostic and ethical rule

The theory is not a diagnostic instrument. Do not infer a person's childhood, topology, diagnosis, motives, or moral character from a single behavior. Narcissistic traits, pathological narcissism, and narcissistic personality disorder are not interchangeable. Similar behavior can emerge from different architectures, and similar architecture can produce different behavior across contexts.

Developmental explanation does not erase adult responsibility. Understanding why a strategy formed does not make harm acceptable.

## 2. Project identity and guiding question

### 2.1 Primary question

> **How does a human being become capable of maintaining a coherent sense of self while remaining genuinely connected to others?**

Engineering formulation:

> **How does an initially externally supported representational system become capable of carrying its own continuity while still participating in reciprocal regulation with other systems?**

### 2.2 Core definition

> **Psychological autonomy is the capacity to maintain one's own psychological wholeness while needing others without losing one's separateness.**

Canonical Finnish formulation:

> **Psykologinen autonomia on kyky ylläpitää oma psykologinen kokonaisuutensa samalla, kun tarvitsee muita menettämättä kokemusta omasta erillisyydestään.**

Autonomy is not independence, invulnerability, isolation, lack of need, or freedom from co-regulation.

> **Healthy development does not eliminate dependency. It transforms its architecture.**

The developmental direction is:

~~~text
You maintain much of my organization.
        ↓
We increasingly maintain it together.
        ↓
I increasingly maintain and recover it myself.
        ↓
We can co-regulate without owning each other's continuity.
~~~

### 2.3 Paradigmatic shift

The project began as an attempt to explain narcissistic regulation. It developed into a theory of regulation and then into a broader theory of psychological autonomy.

Narcissistic organization is now:

- a developmental branch;
- a stress test for the theory;
- and one application of the architecture.

It is not the theory's definition of all psychological difficulty.

Compatible formulations of growth include:

- the capacity to carry more reality;
- the capacity to carry more contradiction;
- the capacity to maintain more differentiated representations simultaneously;
- the capacity to update a model without losing ontological continuity;
- and the transition from externally carried toward increasingly self-carried psychological organization.

## 3. Canonical architecture

### 3.1 Basic processing sequence

~~~text
Reality
  ↓
Signal occurrence and detection
  ↓
Salience weighting
  ↓
Meaning assignment
  ↓
Self-relevance weighting
  ↓
Representations / state estimates
  ↓
Representational organization (topology)
  ↓
Interpretation, regulation, and updating
  ↓
Action
  ↓
New reality data
  ↺
~~~

The system does not act on reality without mediation. It acts on an internally constructed, weighted, and continuously revised model.

This sequence does not imply that subjective processing creates truth. Signal existence, psychological importance, assigned meaning, personal relevance, confidence, and truth value remain distinct.

### 3.2 Second-generation SRF

Earlier SRF began with variables such as Internal Regulation Capacity and Structural Flexibility. The current model places a deeper developmental architecture beneath them:

~~~text
Signals
  ↓
Representation
  ↓
Differentiation
  ↓
Simultaneity
  ↓
Integration
  ↓
Psychological wholeness
  ↓
Psychological autonomy
  ↓
Regulatory capacities and strategies
~~~

**Important distinction:**

> **Simultaneity is a proposed mechanism. Psychological wholeness is the continuity experience that sufficiently successful differentiation, simultaneity, and integration can help produce.**

Internal Regulation Capacity may therefore be emergent rather than primitive.

### 3.3 Current reality and remembered reality

The newest version distinguishes two reality-mediation processes:

~~~text
CURRENT REALITY
signals
  ↓
salience weighting
  ↓
what is noticed and granted importance
  ↓
meaning assignment
  ↓
self-relevance weighting
  ↓
representation and interpretation
~~~

~~~text
REMEMBERED REALITY
memory traces, gaps, and contradictions
  ↓
reconstruction
  ↓
narrative
~~~

These can form a feedback loop:

~~~text
biased salience
  ↓
biased encoding and interpretation
  ↓
selective or distorted remembering
  ↓
defensively coherent narrative
  ↓
stronger prior
  ↓
the same salience distribution is reinforced
~~~

**[HYPOTHESIS]** In some vulnerable or narcissistically organized states, self-relevant threat, criticism, admiration, rejection, or status signals may receive disproportionate salience before explicit defensive reconstruction occurs.

This is not the same as deliberate lying. The person's experienced data may already be asymmetrically weighted.

### 3.4 Salience, meaning assignment, self-relevance, and signs

#### Epistemic status

- **[ESTABLISHED]** Salience is a recognized psychological and neuroscientific concept concerning which stimuli, events, or internal states attract attention and receive motivational or affective importance.
- **[ESTABLISHED]** Apophenia, referential thinking, and ideas of reference are recognized concepts for detecting meaningful patterns or assigning personal reference to events. Their presence, strength, and clinical significance vary widely.
- **[BRIDGE]** Human Kernel uses these concepts to distinguish several functions that may interact but should not be treated as identical.
- **[HYPOTHESIS]** The exact layered architecture below, its feedback dynamics, and any specific connection to narcissistic organization are Human Kernel/SRF proposals rather than established findings.

Salience answers a limited question:

> **Which available signals receive enough psychological importance to enter the foreground?**

It does not by itself specify what a signal means, whether it concerns the observer, or whether an interpretation is true. Human Kernel therefore separates the following stages:

~~~text
1. SIGNAL EXISTENCE / DETECTION
Did an event or pattern occur and was it detected?

2. SALIENCE
How strongly does it stand out or feel important?

3. MEANING ASSIGNMENT
What is the event interpreted as meaning?

4. SELF-RELEVANCE
Is that meaning represented as concerning me, us, or my life?

5. EPISTEMIC EVALUATION
What evidence supports the interpretation, how confident should I be,
and what is its truth value?
~~~

The fifth stage must not be inferred from the preceding four. Truth value is not produced by felt importance, assigned meaning, personal relevance, or confidence. It depends on correspondence with reality and available evidence.

#### How a coincidence becomes a subjectively meaningful sign

A random or ordinary signal can become psychologically powerful without the external event itself changing:

~~~text
11:11 appears on a clock
  ↓
the repeated pattern receives high salience
  ↓
meaning assignment: "this is not an ordinary coincidence"
  ↓
self-relevance: "this sign is meant for me"
  ↓
representation: "the world is communicating with me"
~~~

The same sequence can occur when letters on a license plate resemble a familiar name, or when an animal's appearance is interpreted as symbolizing or embodying a deceased person. The subjective meaning may be emotionally real and important while the causal or metaphysical interpretation remains a separate truth claim.

#### Prior-driven salience feedback

A prior such as **“the world sends me signs”** can train attention and salience toward confirmatory coincidences:

~~~text
PRIOR: "The world sends me signs"
  ↓
attention becomes more sensitive to possible matches
  ↓
matching numbers, letters, or symbols receive high salience
  ↓
meaning and self-relevance are assigned
  ↓
the coincidence is encoded and remembered as confirmation
  ↓
the prior strengthens
  ↺
~~~

Meanwhile, ordinary times, non-matching license plates, common animal appearances, and other non-confirming events receive little salience and fade into the background. The system therefore retains vivid confirming cases while losing much of the comparison set. This is a salience-mediated selection and confirmation loop, not evidence that the prior is true.

#### Scope and non-specificity

This pattern is not specific to narcissism. It can occur in ordinary superstition, spirituality, grief and continuing-bond experiences, stress, sleep deprivation, substance use, mania, psychosis, and other contexts. These contexts must not be treated as equivalent: symbolic meaning-making can be culturally shared, personally comforting, transient, or clinically impairing depending on conviction, flexibility, distress, functional effects, and the wider state of the person.

If narcissistic organization is discussed, the permitted formulation is limited:

> **[HYPOTHESIS] In some narcissistically organized states, self-relevant signals may receive increased weight, making personally referential interpretations more likely under particular conditions.**

Do not state that narcissism causes apophenia, ideas of reference, or sign-seeking. The proposed increase in self-relevance weighting is an SRF/Human Kernel hypothesis and would require independent empirical support.

## 4. Working axioms

These are modeling commitments, not proven psychological laws.

### Axiom A — Mediated access

The psychological system acts on signals and internal estimates, not on reality directly.

### Axiom B — Representational construction

The system constructs dynamic models of self, other people, relationships, the body, values, threats, time, and possible futures.

### Axiom C — Stateful operation

The system's next transition depends on both incoming information and its preceding state.

### Axiom D — Organized dependency

Representations form a network of weighted dependencies. The same representational contents can produce different functioning when their dependency directions differ.

### Axiom E — Limited resources

Maintaining uncertainty, emotional activation, multiple interpretations, and contradiction consumes capacity. Capacity is finite and state-dependent.

### Axiom F — Load-dependent resolution

Representational resolution, simultaneity, and updateability may deteriorate selectively under load. A person can function at high resolution in one domain and low resolution in attachment-relevant states.

### Axiom G — Differentiation before autonomous coordination

Autonomy requires self, other, relationship, action, state, value, and existence to become sufficiently differentiable while remaining capable of coordination.

### Axiom H — Update versus protection

When new information can be integrated without catastrophic structural consequences, the system can update. When updating threatens continuity or a structurally central representation, the system may protect the existing organization by compressing, switching worlds, reallocating badness, externalizing regulation, changing salience, reconstructing memory, or rejecting information.

### Axiom I — Structural consequences

> **Representations are preserved in proportion to the structural consequences of their loss.**

The strength of a defense may reveal not how true a representation is, but how much of the system depends on it.

### Axiom J — Conditions of possibility

Some representations are not merely important contents. They function as **conditions of possibility** for other representations.

Example:

~~~text
I exist
  ↓
I can have value
  ↓
I can succeed, fail, love, repair, and have a future
~~~

Fragile dependency order:

~~~text
I succeed / receive approval
  ↓
I have value
  ↓
I am allowed to exist
~~~

The same nodes appear in both networks; the direction of dependency changes the psychology.

### Axiom K — Resilience as architecture

Resilience depends on error localization, redundancy, modularity, multiple anchors, flexible routing, recovery, and protection against cascading failure—not merely positive beliefs.

### Axiom L — Connection through bounded access

Mature attachment allows real influence and co-regulation without giving another person unlimited write access to the self-system.

### Axiom M — Developmental path dependence

Each successful regulatory strategy changes the starting state of the next event. Repeated short-term solutions can become attractors even when they later create large costs.

## 5. Core constructs

### 5.1 Human kernel

**[METAPHOR/HYPOTHESIS]** The human kernel is the minimal protected continuity that allows a person to experience:

~~~text
I exist.
I am the subject of this experience.
My state can change without ending me.
Another person's state can change without defining my existence.
~~~

It is not:

- a literal brain module;
- the whole personality;
- an immortal essence;
- an unchanging “true self”;
- or a claim that healthy people are unaffected by relationships.

The kernel names the proposed invariants that make flexible change possible.

### 5.2 Signal

A signal is incoming or internally activated information: perception, interoception, language, memory, another person's behavior, social feedback, anticipation, or affective change.

A signal is not its interpretation.

~~~text
Signal:
My partner has not answered for three hours.

Interpretation:
I have been abandoned.
~~~

### 5.3 Representation and representational state

A representation is a dynamic internal model or state estimate concerning self, other, relationship, threat, value, future, or safety.

It may contain:

- estimated current state;
- predicted transitions;
- causal assumptions;
- confidence;
- salience;
- uncertainty;
- temporal continuity;
- regulatory meaning;
- and links to other representations.

### 5.4 Differentiation

Differentiation is the capacity to preserve distinctions such as:

- self versus other;
- person versus current state;
- relationship versus either participant;
- action versus identity;
- guilt versus global shame;
- need versus love;
- value versus performance;
- feeling versus fact;
- salience versus truth;
- influence versus total responsibility;
- and past template versus present person.

### 5.5 Simultaneity

Simultaneity is the capacity to keep differentiated but conflicting representations active at the same time:

~~~text
I need you
AND
I remain separate from you.

You hurt me
AND
you remain a whole person.

I acted badly
AND
I remain capable of responsibility and repair.
~~~

**[HYPOTHESIS]** When simultaneity fails under emotional load, the system becomes more likely to collapse toward one low-cost interpretation or to switch between incompatible world-organizations.

Splitting should not automatically be described as an active operation that cuts one representation into two. Within this model it may be better understood as the failure to maintain contradictory aspects simultaneously in one sufficiently continuous model.

### 5.6 Integration

Integration is the formation of relations among differentiated representations without erasing their differences. It is not mere intellectual knowledge that two propositions are true. The architecture must preserve both under relevant load.

### 5.7 Psychological wholeness

Psychological wholeness is continuity of self across changing states. It does not mean consistency, purity, permanent well-being, or lack of conflict.

Canonical relation:

~~~text
differentiation
  ↓
simultaneity
  ↓
integration
  ↓
psychological wholeness
~~~

Wholeness is the experiential and functional outcome; simultaneity is one proposed enabling mechanism.

### 5.8 Ontological continuity

“Ontological” is used psychologically:

> **I continue to exist as the same subject through state change.**

It is not a metaphysical claim. An ontological threat is an event represented as endangering one's right or capacity to continue as a self.

### 5.9 Resolution

Representational resolution is the amount of relevant variation a model can preserve and use.

~~~text
Low resolution:
person → good OR bad

Higher resolution:
person → caring, tired, angry, mistaken, independent,
         continuous through time, capable of harm and repair
~~~

Resolution is domain- and state-specific. Sophisticated intellectual functioning does not guarantee high-resolution self–other modeling under attachment threat.

### 5.10 Weight, authority, salience, meaning, self-relevance, evidence, confidence, and truth

These variables must not be collapsed:

~~~text
Resolution:
How much complexity does the representation preserve?

Weight / authority:
How much does this representation control inference and action?

Salience:
How strongly does this signal attract attention and feel significant?

Meaning assignment:
What is the signal interpreted as meaning?

Self-relevance:
How strongly is that meaning represented as concerning me, us, or my life?

Evidence:
How much data supports the interpretation?

Confidence:
How certain is the system?

Truth:
How accurately does the model correspond to reality?
~~~

Signal existence does not imply psychological importance. High salience does not imply assigned meaning, personal relevance, high evidence, or truth. Personal relevance does not establish causal relevance. A low-resolution representation can have high authority. A person's self-model may be richly described yet automatically lose epistemic weight when another person disagrees.

### 5.11 Self–other weighting asymmetry

**[HYPOTHESIS]** The self-sacrifice and other-sacrifice branches may involve opposite asymmetries:

~~~text
SELF-SACRIFICE / HYPERVIGILANT ORGANIZATION
self resolution or authority ↓
other monitoring and authority ↑

OTHER-SACRIFICE / NARCISSISTIC DEFENSIVE ORGANIZATION
self-relevant model authority ↑
other-person resolution or authority ↓ under load
~~~

In the first pattern, another person's interpretation may automatically outrank one's own experience. In the second, the other's independent subjectivity may be subordinated to its effect on self-regulation.

This is dimensional, not a classification of people into two types.

### 5.12 Structural centrality, keystone representations, and conditions of possibility

Structural centrality is the extent to which the availability of other representations depends on one representation.

A keystone representation is a node whose loss or reversal produces disproportionate disruption.

The key question is:

> **What else becomes impossible if this representation disappears?**

Examples of possible keystone representations:

- “I exist.”
- “I am lovable.”
- “My caregiver is good.”
- “I am innocent.”
- “This relationship cannot end.”
- “God sees me.”
- “This regulator is necessary for me to function.”

The centrality of a representation is an empirical question, not something inferred merely from how passionately it is stated.

### 5.13 Compression

Compression is a low-resolution outcome in which relevant distinctions or alternatives are lost.

~~~text
High resolution:
Several explanations remain possible.

Compressed:
They rejected me.
~~~

Crucial revision:

> **Compression may be the observable result, not the deepest mechanism.**

Proposed causal order:

~~~text
load or structural threat
  ↓
differentiation or simultaneity fails
  ↓
alternatives cannot remain active
  ↓
representational resolution decreases
  ↓
compression becomes visible
~~~

Compression is universally necessary in many contexts. The problem is rigid, identity-protective, attachment-specific, or non-recovering compression.

### 5.14 Defensive reconstruction and confabulation

Compression removes distinctions. Defensive reconstruction changes meaning, causal allocation, temporal narrative, or represented identity so a protected structure can survive.

Possible forms include:

- selective remembering;
- causal reassignment;
- certainty replacing uncertainty;
- idealization and devaluation;
- projection;
- switching active world-models;
- and confabulation.

Not every inaccurate memory is defensive, and confabulation should not be used as a casual synonym for lying.

### 5.15 Internal Regulation Capacity

Internal Regulation Capacity (IRC) is the capacity to sustain activation, ambiguity, and multiple representations without immediate global disorganization or compulsory external regulation.

In current SRF, IRC is provisionally emergent from:

- differentiation;
- simultaneity;
- available cognitive and affective resources;
- robust topology;
- internalized regulation;
- and recovery history.

### 5.16 Structural Flexibility

Structural Flexibility (SF) is the capacity to revise and reorganize representations while preserving overall continuity.

Flexibility is not instability. It means local change need not become global collapse.

### 5.17 External Regulation Dependence

External Regulation Dependence (ERD) is the degree to which coherence or recovery depends on feedback, reassurance, admiration, control, submission, status, a relationship, a substance, a ritual, or another external regulator.

Dependence is not itself pathology. The key distinction is:

~~~text
external source participates in regulation
versus
external source becomes a condition of continuity
~~~

### 5.18 Regulation Externalization Latency and Externalization Vectors

Regulation Externalization Latency (REL) is the proposed delay between rising dysregulation and an attempt to solve it through the environment.

Externalization Vectors (EV) are preferred routes, such as:

- reassurance;
- admiration;
- pleasing;
- submission;
- control;
- attack;
- withdrawal;
- performance;
- projection;
- substances;
- compulsive certainty;
- and relationship recruitment.

The same behavior can have different functions in different architectures.

### 5.19 Dynamic Stability Profile

The Dynamic Stability Profile (DSP) is a proposed description of:

- baseline organization;
- activation threshold;
- loss-of-resolution threshold;
- simultaneity failure;
- world-switching threshold;
- externalization latency and direction;
- peak disorganization;
- recovery time;
- return of resolution;
- and whether recovery restores, revises, or merely reasserts the old model.

### 5.20 Developmental Resolution Injury Hypothesis

The **Developmental Resolution Injury Hypothesis (DRIH)** proposes that developmentally insufficient or adverse conditions can leave particular representational domains unable to sustain differentiated, high-resolution processing under relevant load.

It does not claim:

- one event creates a fixed personality;
- all difficulties originate in parenting;
- or low resolution is global.

Temperament, repeated interaction, learning, trauma, culture, later relationships, and current load may all matter.

### 5.21 Developmental Irreversibility Hypothesis

The **Developmental Irreversibility Hypothesis (DIH)** is a separate, open hypothesis:

> **Some architectures or capacities that normally emerge during a developmental window may not later be reconstructible in exactly the same form, even when substantial functional recovery is possible.**

Do not use the strong claim “early topology can never change” as an axiom. Distinguish:

1. reorganization of the original structure;
2. construction of a functionally equivalent new structure;
3. compensation through alternative routes;
4. symptom or behavior change without a change in core dependencies.

These are competing models of recovery. The current theory does not choose among them.

### 5.22 Resolution Resistance and the J-curve

Resolution Resistance is the proposed tendency to resist representational detail when greater detail initially threatens a stabilizing organization.

The J-curve hypothesis proposes that increasing resolution may first increase distress before a more integrated organization becomes possible.

Both are hypotheses, not established laws of therapy.

### 5.23 Process isolation

**Process isolation [METAPHOR/HYPOTHESIS]** is the degree to which self, other, and relationship states can influence one another without becoming the same variable.

Canonical formulation:

> **Your state can change my state without becoming the definition of my state or identity.**

Low isolation:

~~~text
other disapproves
  ↓
self becomes globally bad
  ↓
relationship becomes destroyed
  ↓
future becomes impossible
~~~

Higher isolation:

~~~text
other disapproves
  ↓
self is affected
  ↓
meaning remains uncertain and local
  ↓
self, other, relationship, and future can update separately
~~~

Isolation is not emotional detachment. A system with zero permeability cannot love or co-regulate; a system with no isolation is vulnerable to overwrite. Mature autonomy requires bounded permeability.

### 5.24 The internal model as a digital twin

**Digital twin [METAPHOR]** describes the mind's continuously revised model of self, others, and world:

~~~text
reality
  ↓ signals, outcomes, prediction errors
internal model / "digital twin"
  ↓ prediction and action
new reality data
  ↺
~~~

A healthy internal model is not permanently correct. It is corrigible: it can absorb prediction error without the whole system losing continuity. A protected model reverses this relation and increasingly requires reality to conform to it.

## 6. Boot sequence: how the system starts

**[ESTABLISHED/BRIDGE]** A young child begins biologically active, responsive, and capable of rudimentary self–other discrimination, but remains deeply dependent on caregiver-supported regulation.

The child is not an empty device, and caregiver and child do not literally share one mind. The functional claim is:

> **At first, part of the maintenance of the child's psychological organization occurs through processes outside the child's individual boundaries.**

Caregiver-supported regulation forms a reciprocal but asymmetric loop:

~~~text
child state
  ↓
signal
  ↓
caregiver perception and interpretation
  ↓
caregiver response
  ↓
child's new state
  ↺
~~~

The loop is reciprocal because the child affects the caregiver. It is asymmetric because responsibility for maintaining the relationship does not belong to the infant.

Repeated regulation may help build expectations such as:

- states have beginnings and endings;
- distress can change;
- a signal may reach another person;
- connection can return after rupture;
- my action has effects without controlling everything;
- the caregiver's state is not a direct definition of me;
- and I need not earn existence by regulating the caregiver.

The boot sequence is not completed in infancy. These dependencies are renegotiated throughout development and adult attachment.

## 7. The core developmental contradiction

The child needs and loves a caregiver who also frustrates, fails, limits, withdraws, or sometimes hurts.

~~~text
The caregiver is loved and needed
AND
the caregiver frustrates, fails, or hurts.

The child loves the caregiver
AND
the child feels anger, aggression, disappointment, or hatred.
~~~

“Hurts” includes ordinary frustration and caregiver fallibility; abuse is not required by the model, although severe adversity may intensify the problem.

Integration allows:

~~~text
You hurt me
AND
you remain a whole person.

I hurt you
AND
I remain a whole person capable of responsibility.
~~~

When differentiation and simultaneity do not carry the contradiction, the system may stabilize with:

~~~text
good caregiver
OR
bad caregiver

good self
OR
bad self
~~~

This resolves contradiction by reducing the model rather than integrating the data.

## 8. Conditional caregiver switching and the illusion of control

The newest developmental hypothesis adds a control function to the dualistic world.

In a sufficiently safe relationship, the child can learn:

~~~text
My behavior affects the caregiver's response.
AND
the caregiver remains the same person.
AND
the relationship can survive.
~~~

In a conditionally responsive or experienced-as-unpredictable relationship, the learned rule may become:

~~~text
right behavior
  ↓
good caregiver appears
  ↓
connection and safety return

wrong behavior
  ↓
good caregiver disappears
  ↓
bad or absent caregiver appears
  ↓
connection is endangered
~~~

Two routes must be distinguished:

1. **Real conditionality:** warmth, approval, or closeness actually varies with compliance, performance, emotion, or the image the child provides.
2. **Apparent control:** the caregiver's state is driven largely by stress, shame, fatigue, trauma, relationship problems, or other hidden variables, but the child observes only “I acted; the caregiver changed.”

The child's inference may overfit visible causality:

~~~text
caregiver changed
  ↓
what did I just do?
  ↓
I caused the change
~~~

The conclusion is painful but offers an advantage over helplessness:

> **If I caused the bad caregiver, I may be able to restore the good caregiver by changing myself.**

Possible control policies:

- be good;
- become perfect;
- please;
- succeed;
- need less;
- hide the wrong emotion;
- anticipate the caregiver;
- regulate the caregiver;
- disappear when inconvenient.

### Dual-world control hypothesis

> **[HYPOTHESIS] The good and bad caregiver may remain separated not only because the child cannot integrate them, but because their separation preserves the illusion that the child can control which world becomes active.**

~~~text
own behavior
  ↓
caregiver state
  ↓
active world
  ↓
availability of connection
  ↓
own safety and continuity
~~~

This mechanism is not uniquely narcissistic. It may contribute to perfectionism, pleasing, hypervigilance, anxious attachment, compulsive responsibility, control, or difficulty knowing one's own needs.

## 9. Guilt, shame, badness, and existence

The model must distinguish four phenomena:

- **guilt:** “I did something harmful to someone I value.”
- **responsibility:** proportionate ownership of an action, contribution, or repair duty.
- **shame:** painful negative evaluation or exposure of the self.
- **global badness:** the entire self or other is represented as the bad object/node.

Adaptive guilt localizes error:

~~~text
I did harm
  ↓
I remain a continuing person
  ↓
I can bear concern
  ↓
I can repair
  ↓
the model can update
~~~

Globalized badness produces:

~~~text
I did harm
  ↓
I am entirely bad
  ↓
badness must disappear
  ↓
my right or capacity to exist is threatened
~~~

The key ontological distinction is:

~~~text
INTEGRATED ORGANIZATION
existence precedes moral evaluation

I exist
  ↓
I can contain good and bad action, impulse, and feeling

DUALISTIC ORGANIZATION
moral evaluation can become a condition of existence

I am good
  ↓
I am allowed to exist
~~~

**[HYPOTHESIS]** Some narcissistic defenses may protect not merely against a negative self-image but against the ontological conclusion activated when badness enters a self-model that cannot integrate it.

If badness can be located in the other:

~~~text
you are bad
  ↓
I remain good
  ↓
I remain allowed to exist
~~~

If badness is forced into the self:

~~~text
I am bad
  ↓
badness should not exist
  ↓
I should not exist
~~~

This is a proposed structural logic, not a claim that every narcissistically organized person consciously thinks this way or becomes suicidal.

## 10. Developmental bifurcation

### 10.1 Canonical bifurcation

When the contradiction cannot yet be integrated, the system may stabilize by globalizing badness predominantly toward self or other:

~~~text
          CAREGIVER = LOVE + FRUSTRATION/HURT
          SELF = LOVE + ANGER/AGGRESSION
                         │
                         ▼
       contradiction cannot yet be integrated
                         │
                ┌────────┴────────┐
                │                 │
                ▼                 ▼
        SELF-SACRIFICE      OTHER-SACRIFICE
          "I am bad"          "You are bad"
        preserve other      preserve self
        and attachment      and self-coherence
~~~

Integration is absent from this bifurcation by definition. The node is reached only after the system is currently unable to integrate the contradiction.

### 10.2 Self-sacrifice

~~~text
caregiver frustrates, fails, or withdraws
  ↓
"caregiver is bad/unsafe" predicts attachment danger
  ↓
"I am the problem" preserves the needed caregiver
  ↓
monitor, please, submit, suppress need and anger
  ↓
conflict or withdrawal decreases
  ↓
strategy is reinforced
~~~

Possible later correlates:

- global self-blame;
- shame;
- hypervigilance to others' states;
- pleasing and submission;
- need suppression;
- low self-model authority;
- over-assumption of responsibility;
- and vulnerability to exploitative relationships.

### 10.3 Other-sacrifice

~~~text
frustration, shame, criticism, or failure
  ↓
self-implication predicts identity or ontological danger
  ↓
"you are the problem"
  ↓
blame, devaluation, control, entitlement, or projection
  ↓
other yields, apologizes, admires, or restores regulation
  ↓
distress decreases
  ↓
strategy is reinforced
~~~

Possible later correlates:

- externalized responsibility;
- binary judgments;
- devaluation;
- control of others as regulators;
- shame sensitivity;
- admiration seeking;
- entitlement;
- and some narcissistic defenses.

This branch is not equivalent to NPD.

### 10.4 Integration is outside the defensive bifurcation

~~~text
contradiction is encountered
  ↓
integration is currently possible
  ↓
contradictory experience
  ↓
both sides remain active
  ↓
affect is regulated
  ↓
action is separated from identity
  ↓
responsibility is differentiated
  ↓
repair and model update
  ↓
higher-resolution self–other organization
~~~

Integration is the developmental alternative that prevents entry into the defensive bifurcation, or a later exit that becomes possible if regulation, differentiation, and simultaneity improve. It is not a third solution available at the point where integration has already been ruled out.

Integration does not require forgiveness, equal responsibility, denial of abuse, or staying in a harmful relationship. It permits accurate asymmetry without making either person a totalized metaphysical bad object.

### 10.5 The two defensive branches are attractors, not types

The model is probabilistic and state-dependent. A person may:

- self-sacrifice with powerful people and other-sacrifice with vulnerable people;
- self-blame at baseline and externalize under overload;
- defend grandiosely and later collapse into global shame;
- use different routes in different relationships;
- or leave the bifurcation as integration becomes possible over time.

“Branch” means a recurrent probability distribution shaped by temperament, learning, current load, power, and relational feedback.

### 10.6 Nature and nurture

~~~text
temperament / partly heritable parameters
        +
caregiver and network response distribution
        +
blame and responsibility routing
        +
developmental reinforcement history
        ↓
is integration currently possible?
        │
        ├── YES → integration; no defensive bifurcation
        │
        └── NO  → defensive bifurcation
                    ├── self-sacrifice attractor
                    └── other-sacrifice attractor
~~~

Heritability does not imply a fully formed narcissistic topology is inherited. Candidate inherited parameters may include reactivity, inhibition, reward sensitivity, dominance, shame sensitivity, impulsivity, and susceptibility to environmental influence.

## 11. Blame–Responsibility Market

### 11.1 Conceptual and formal levels

The **Blame–Responsibility Market** is the project's compact metaphor for a family or relational network in which contradiction, badness, blame, guilt, and responsibility are repeatedly allocated.

The terminology must separate:

- **felt guilt** inside a person;
- **blame allocation** between people;
- **global badness assignment** to a self or other representation;
- and **responsibility distribution** for specific actions.

The formal construct beneath the market metaphor is:

> **Blame–Responsibility Routing Network**

### 11.2 Core proposition

> **[HYPOTHESIS] A child's stabilizing branch depends partly on where the relational network permits negative responsibility, accusation, aggression, and global badness to be routed—and what regulatory result follows each route.**

The central question is:

> **Who can carry the guilt, blame, or badness without the relationship or a person collapsing?**

### 11.3 Four canonical market outcomes

~~~text
A. Child carries global badness
   → caregiver and attachment remain good
   → self-sacrifice can stabilize

B. Caregiver accepts global badness and restores regulation
   → child's externalization succeeds
   → other-sacrifice can stabilize

C. Nobody can carry badness
   → escalation, chaotic switching, retaliation, or rigid splitting

D. Global badness is replaced by differentiated responsibility
   → actions are localized
   → integration becomes learnable
~~~

These are network-level response patterns, not branches of the defensive bifurcation. Outcome D changes the conditions so that the system may avoid or later leave the two-branch bifurcation.

The integrated response can say:

> “I was wrong to shout at you, and you are responsible for hitting. Neither action requires either of us to become entirely bad.”

Responsibility can be unequal and still differentiated.

### 11.4 Caregiver response distribution

Development depends on a distribution, not one event or one caregiver. The relevant network may include:

- parents and other primary caregivers;
- siblings and extended family;
- peers and teachers;
- institutions;
- cultural norms about obedience, anger, status, guilt, and repair;
- and the child's power position in the system.

Do not turn “the hypervigilant mother” into a privileged universal cause. Caregiver gender is not a theoretical variable.

Relevant response classes include:

- attunement and co-regulation;
- accepting real responsibility;
- accepting false or global blame;
- retaliation or humiliation;
- withdrawal or attachment threat;
- capitulation and loss of boundaries;
- overvaluation or exceptional treatment;
- consistent limits without humiliation;
- curiosity and mentalization;
- repair;
- differentiated redistribution of responsibility;
- and unpredictable alternation.

### 11.5 Three operational caregiver capacities

An earlier candidate variable, **Blame Absorption Capacity**, was too ambiguous. Decompose it:

1. **Affective Containment Capacity (ACC) [OPERATIONAL]**  
   Capacity to tolerate the child's anger, accusation, or distress without retaliating, collapsing, abandoning the relationship, or forcing immediate relief.

2. **Undifferentiated Blame Assumption (UBA) [OPERATIONAL]**  
   Tendency to accept global or inaccurate blame and surrender boundaries to restore calm.

3. **Responsibility Redistribution Capacity (RRC) [OPERATIONAL]**  
   Capacity to acknowledge one's real share, reject false global badness, return the child's share without humiliation, and support repair.

Proposed healthy profile:

~~~text
ACC high
UBA low
RRC high
~~~

High ACC is not the same as accepting all blame. Low UBA is not defensiveness. High RRC allows responsibility to be accurate without making anyone disappear.

### 11.6 Reinforcement loops

Other-sacrifice reinforcement:

~~~text
child externalizes blame
  ↓
caregiver accepts global bad role, yields, or over-apologizes
  ↓
caregiver restores the child's regulation
  ↓
distress falls
  ↓
externalization becomes more probable
~~~

Self-sacrifice reinforcement:

~~~text
child protests or blames caregiver
  ↓
caregiver retaliates, shames, or withdraws
  ↓
externalization becomes dangerous
  ↓
child redirects badness toward self
  ↓
connection or calm returns
  ↓
self-sacrifice becomes more probable
~~~

Integration reinforcement:

~~~text
rupture
  ↓
affect is contained
  ↓
responsibility is localized and distributed
  ↓
neither self nor other becomes globally bad
  ↓
repair restores connection
  ↓
integration becomes more probable
~~~

### 11.7 What the “market” metaphor adds

The metaphor directs attention to:

- **available carriers:** who is willing or forced to become the bad node;
- **transfer cost:** what happens when blame moves toward self or other;
- **regulatory yield:** how much immediate relief the transfer produces;
- **power:** who can refuse blame and who cannot;
- **liquidity:** how easily badness moves between people;
- **concentration:** whether one person becomes the permanent scapegoat;
- **inflation:** whether increasingly large accusations are required for the same regulatory relief;
- and **conversion:** whether global blame can become differentiated responsibility and repair.

These are metaphoric prompts, not validated economic variables. Formal work should model reinforcement, routing, cost, power, and state change directly.

### 11.8 Testable predictions

The market model predicts, provisionally:

- repeated caregiver capitulation contingent on accusation should strengthen externalizing regulation more than empathic containment with maintained boundaries;
- retaliatory withdrawal contingent on protest should strengthen self-blame and protest inhibition;
- high ACC plus high RRC should predict better action–identity differentiation;
- the branch reinforced in one power relationship may reverse in another;
- immediate distress reduction should mediate reinforcement of the chosen blame route;
- and global blame allocation should predict lower later model updateability than differentiated responsibility.

## 12. Narcissistic organization as an application

The cautious central hypothesis is:

> **Under increasing regulation load, another person's function as a regulator may displace the higher-resolution representation of that person as an independent subject.**

The governing question shifts from:

> Who are you?

to:

> What are you doing to my state?

The other may become an admirer, rejecter, rescuer, rival, offender, extension, or threat. When the function changes, the active world may change.

### 12.1 Two poorly integrated worlds

~~~text
GOOD WORLD
self valuable
other safe or admiring
relationship secure
future possible

BAD WORLD
self worthless or threatened
other hostile or contemptible
relationship destroyed
future collapsed
~~~

A small signal may not update one node. It may switch the entire active configuration.

This “two-world model” is a metaphor and hypothesis, not a universal property of everyone with an NPD diagnosis.

### 12.2 The self-maintaining hell

The phrase “stuck in one's own hell” can be translated mechanistically:

~~~text
rigid prior
  ↓
threat-biased salience
  ↓
ambiguous event interpreted as insult, abandonment, or opposition
  ↓
defensive action
  ↓
relationship conflict
  ↓
new data appears to confirm the prior
~~~

The defenses protect the system from immediate collapse while making the experienced world increasingly threatening. The “hell” is self-maintaining, not imaginary in the sense of painless: defensive behavior can create real relational consequences.

### 12.3 Dual Mothership as an extension

**[EXTENSION]** In a mutually dependent or “dual mothership” relationship, both adults may recruit the other as an idealized, unconditional regulator.

~~~text
Self A continuity ← regulation supplied by B
Self B continuity ← regulation supplied by A
~~~

The relationship then does more than connect two autonomous systems. It partly replaces missing internal regulation for both. Boundaries, disagreement, and separateness become dangerous because they interrupt regulatory supply.

This is an SRF interpretation of an adjacent model, not established clinical doctrine.

## 13. Runtime modification and external regulators

### 13.1 General principle

A person does not necessarily attempt to change reality or consciously edit representations when using a regulator. They may change the **runtime state** in which reality is weighted, interpreted, associated, and felt.

~~~text
same external reality
  +
different runtime
  =
different experienced world
~~~

Candidate runtime modifiers include:

- alcohol and other substances;
- reassurance;
- admiration;
- control;
- compulsive action;
- status;
- ritual;
- sleep deprivation;
- acute stress;
- medication;
- and intense relational contact.

### 13.2 Negative reinforcement

~~~text
distress
  ↓
external regulator
  ↓
state changes
  ↓
distress falls
  ↓
regulator becomes more probable and structurally important
~~~

Over time, representations may form that protect access to the regulator:

~~~text
"I need this to think."
"This is the only thing that calms me."
"Others do not understand."
~~~

Such representations may be partly true, partly learned, or partly defensive. Their status must be investigated rather than assumed.

### 13.3 Alcohol as a capacity stress test

**[OPERATIONAL]** Acute alcohol exposure may be useful in a properly approved research design because it can lower inhibition, working memory, ambiguity tolerance, and social-cognitive capacity. The theoretical question is:

> **Does controlled reduction of available capacity decrease representational simultaneity and increase categorical social interpretation?**

Possible measures:

- representational breadth;
- interpretation entropy;
- hostile attribution;
- self-versus-other blame weighting;
- confidence;
- response latency;
- model update after disconfirming evidence;
- and resolution collapse from sober to altered state.

The strongest early design may be within-subject:

~~~text
same participant
session A: sober/placebo
session B: controlled alcohol condition
social ambiguity and update task
~~~

This requires ethics review, medical safety, calibrated dosing, consent, exclusions, and sober discharge procedures. People with alcohol dependence should not be treated as a cheap or convenient test population. Chronic use introduces major confounds and clinical risk.

### 13.4 Cannabis and salience

**[EXTENSION]** Cannabis may alter salience, association, time experience, interoception, memory processing, anxiety, and threat weighting. It need not increase representational resolution even when experience feels deeper.

> **Experienced depth is not the same as reality fidelity.**

### 13.5 Psychedelics and topological flexibility

**[EXTENSION]** Psychedelic states may temporarily loosen dominant priors and make alternative representational connections experientially available.

Do not call them a “topology scanner.” A highly salient insight is not automatically true.

~~~text
experienced significance ↑
does not imply
epistemic accuracy ↑
~~~

The relevant research question is whether temporary flexibility followed by careful integration can improve later updateability. The experience itself must be separated from the evidence used to evaluate its interpretation.

## 14. Religion as an ontological architecture

**[EXTENSION]** Human Kernel can analyze religious representations without deciding whether religious claims are metaphysically true or false.

A religious system may provide high-centrality representations that:

- reduce ontological uncertainty;
- organize causality and morality;
- provide meaning;
- preserve continuity in the face of death;
- provide an ultimate witness or regulator;
- and synchronize individual world-models through shared ritual and narrative.

~~~text
God / cosmic order
  ↓
meaning
morality
identity
future
death
community
~~~

“God sees me” may function as an ontological anchor when human recognition fails. A religious community can function as a shared protocol that reduces coordination cost.

These psychological functions neither prove nor disprove the truth of a religion.

The relevant architectural question is updateability:

~~~text
flexible faith + new information
  ↓
interpretation can evolve

rigid central representation + disconfirming information
  ↓
information must be rejected to protect the network
~~~

## 15. Love, attachment, and distributed systems

Love means that another person has real access to our regulation. Mature love is therefore not isolation behind perfect boundaries.

Canonical formulation:

> **Love is granting access to one's regulatory system. Psychological autonomy is retaining the master key.**

The metaphor must not glorify control. Another person can deeply affect us; grief and dependency do not prove lack of autonomy.

Healthy distributed regulation allows:

~~~text
Self A ←→ shared relational state ←→ Self B
~~~

while:

~~~text
Self A remains owned by A
Self B remains owned by B
~~~

The relationship is real and irreducible to either person, but neither party should become the sole condition of the other's right to exist.

## 16. Fault tolerance, therapy, and recovery

Fault tolerance means localizing error so one event does not rewrite the entire self, other, relationship, and future.

~~~text
criticism
  ↓
local update
  ↓
reflection and repair
~~~

versus:

~~~text
criticism
  ↓
global badness
  ↓
world switch
  ↓
attack, collapse, withdrawal, or reconstruction
~~~

Therapy can be described as architectural reconstruction, but several change models remain open:

1. original topology reorganizes;
2. a functionally equivalent new topology develops;
3. alternative routes compensate for the original vulnerability;
4. behavior and symptoms change while core dependencies remain.

Possible therapeutic functions include:

- providing co-regulation without ownership;
- increasing differentiation;
- extending simultaneity under load;
- separating action from identity;
- converting global blame into differentiated responsibility;
- returning epistemic authority to a devalued self-model;
- restoring the other as a person rather than a function;
- widening salience beyond threat-confirming signals;
- testing reconstructions against reality;
- increasing recovery and return of resolution;
- and creating experiences in which rupture does not require sacrifice of self or other.

The model does not yet establish which of these changes is necessary, sufficient, or irreversible.

## 17. Formal and computational sketch

The following variables are conceptual proposals:

- \(L_t\): total cognitive and affective load at time \(t\)
- \(C_t\): currently available processing and regulation capacity
- \(D_t\): differentiation
- \(S_t\): representational simultaneity
- \(R_t\): resolution
- \(W_t\): psychological wholeness/continuity
- \(U_t\): updateability
- \(E_i\): structural consequences of losing representation \(i\)
- \(A_i\): salience assigned to signal or representation \(i\)
- \(M_i\): meaning assigned to signal \(i\)
- \(H_i\): self-relevance weight assigned to signal or meaning \(i\)
- \(P_i\): epistemic authority/weight of representation \(i\)
- \(G_t\): blame–responsibility routing state
- \(X_t\): external regulation demand

### 17.1 Capacity relation

Provisional expectation:

~~~text
if L_t approaches or exceeds C_t:
    simultaneity ↓
    resolution ↓
    categorical interpretation ↑
    external regulation demand ↑
~~~

The relation need not be linear. Domain-specific thresholds and hysteresis are expected.

### 17.2 Structural defense pressure

~~~text
defense pressure for representation i
    increases with
structural consequences E_i
    ×
predicted probability of loss or reversal
~~~

This operationalizes:

> **Representations are preserved in proportion to the structural consequences of their loss.**

### 17.3 Salience, meaning, self-relevance, and evidence

Inference should model salience, assigned meaning, self-relevance, and evidence separately:

~~~text
experienced importance(signal i) = A_i
assigned interpretation(signal i) = M_i
self-relevance(signal i)           = H_i
evidential support(signal i)      = data quality and likelihood
truth value                       = correspondence with reality
~~~

A high \(A_i\) may dominate limited capacity even when evidential support is low. A high \(H_i\) may make one interpretation feel personally compelling without increasing its evidential support. No equation here permits salience, meaning, or self-relevance to determine truth value.

### 17.4 Branch selection

For a given event, candidate strategies may be compared by expected short-term cost:

~~~text
cost(self-sacrifice)
cost(other-sacrifice)
cost(integration)
~~~

Costs depend on:

- attachment threat;
- shame and ontological threat;
- aggression cost;
- cognitive load;
- caregiver/network response probabilities;
- expected regulatory relief;
- current power;
- and learned value of each route.

The lowest short-term cost can become the highest long-term cost.

### 17.5 Blame–Responsibility Routing Graph

Represent agents as nodes. Directed edges represent possible routes for:

- accusation;
- global badness;
- affective containment;
- responsibility;
- repair;
- and regulatory relief.

Each routing event updates the probability of future routes.

The desired emergent test is whether self-sacrifice and other-sacrifice attractors appear from local rules without being explicitly programmed as outcomes.

## 18. Empirical research programme

### 18.1 Minimum falsifiable claim

> **Under increasing attachment-relevant load, some people show coordinated loss of representational simultaneity, reduced resolution, more global self-or-other badness, slower updating, and increased external regulation.**

If these variables do not covary beyond existing measures of affect intensity, cognitive load, attachment insecurity, or personality traits, the theory's added value is weakened.

### 18.2 Candidate tasks

1. Present ambiguous social events.
2. Record first interpretation.
3. Ask for multiple plausible alternatives.
4. Require probability allocation across alternatives.
5. Introduce disconfirming and confirming information.
6. Measure how rapidly and locally the model updates.
7. Repeat under attachment threat, cognitive load, sleep loss, or an ethically approved pharmacological manipulation.

### 18.3 Candidate measures

- **Representational breadth:** number of plausible concurrent interpretations.
- **Interpretation entropy:** distribution of probability across alternatives.
- **Simultaneity:** ability to retain conflicting but compatible propositions.
- **Hostile attribution:** weight given to hostile explanations.
- **Self-blame and other-blame globality:** whether responsibility remains action-specific.
- **Updateability:** degree of change after new evidence.
- **Resolution recovery:** return of complexity after load is removed.
- **World-switch coupling:** coordinated change across self, other, relationship, and future.
- **Salience–evidence dissociation:** subjective significance relative to evidential support.
- **Meaning-assignment rate:** probability that an otherwise ordinary coincidence receives a non-random interpretation.
- **Self-relevance attribution:** probability that an assigned meaning is represented as personally directed or personally significant.
- **Comparison-set retention:** encoding and recall of non-matching events alongside salient matches.
- **Externalization latency:** time before regulation is recruited from the environment.
- **Responsibility differentiation:** proportional allocation without global badness.

### 18.4 Blame–Responsibility Market study

Possible observational or experimental family interaction design:

- code child protest, accusation, self-blame, and distress;
- code caregiver containment, retaliation, withdrawal, capitulation, accurate responsibility, and repair;
- measure immediate child regulation;
- model which route becomes more likely over repeated interactions;
- include multiple caregivers and contexts;
- control for temperament, age, power, adversity, and baseline symptoms.

The theory predicts that the immediate regulatory payoff of a blame route is a key mediator of its reinforcement.

### 18.5 Key falsification criteria

The model is weakened if:

- simultaneity adds no predictive value beyond general executive function;
- structural centrality adds no value beyond emotional importance;
- branch direction is not related to relational response distribution or reinforcement;
- salience can be fully reduced to attention or affect intensity without improving explanation;
- global blame routing does not predict later regulation strategy;
- recovery of resolution is unrelated to autonomy or resilience;
- or proposed variables cannot be operationalized reliably across methods.

- differentiation;

## 24. Compact bootstrap context

Use this when a smaller model cannot ingest the full document:

> Human Kernel is Jani Halmetoja's developing Theory of Psychological Autonomy. It asks how an initially caregiver-regulated child becomes capable of maintaining a continuous sense of self while remaining genuinely connected to others. The kernel is a metaphor for minimal psychological continuity: “I exist; I remain me through changing states; another person's state can affect me without defining my existence.” The model proposes that autonomy depends on differentiation, representational simultaneity, integration, and robust topology. Simultaneity is the proposed mechanism; psychological wholeness is the continuity experience it helps make possible. Representations are defended in proportion to the structural consequences of their loss, especially when they function as conditions of possibility for other representations. Under load, differentiation or simultaneity may fail, producing low-resolution compression, world switching, external regulation, or defensive reconstruction.
>
> The core developmental contradiction is that a needed caregiver is both loving and frustrating, while the child both loves and feels anger. If the contradiction can be integrated, harmful action remains local and guilt can support responsibility, repair, and update. If integration is not currently possible, a defensive bifurcation has two branches: self-sacrifice preserves the caregiver by globalizing badness into the self, while other-sacrifice preserves the self by globalizing badness into the other. The two branches are state-dependent attractors, not diagnoses or permanent types. Integration is not a third branch under the condition that defines the bifurcation.
>
> The Blame–Responsibility Market names the hypothesis that a relational network routes guilt, blame, global badness, responsibility, and regulatory burden. A child may carry global blame, a caregiver may absorb it and reinforce externalization, nobody may tolerate it and produce chaos, or responsibility may be differentiated without making anyone wholly bad. The formal substrate is the Blame–Responsibility Routing Network. Proposed caregiver variables are Affective Containment Capacity, Undifferentiated Blame Assumption, and Responsibility Redistribution Capacity.
>
> A newer control hypothesis proposes that a dualistic good/bad caregiver model can give a child the illusion that correct behavior makes the good caregiver appear and wrong behavior activates the bad caregiver. Self-blame preserves control over an otherwise unpredictable attachment system. This can develop toward pleasing, perfectionism, hypervigilance, control, or—on one branch—narcissistic externalization.
>
> The current architecture separates signal existence, salience, meaning assignment, self-relevance, evidence, confidence, and truth. Salience determines what receives psychological importance; it does not itself specify meaning, personal reference, or truth. Repeated numbers, license-plate letter patterns, or symbolic animal appearances can become subjectively meaningful when high salience is followed by meaning assignment and self-relevance. A prior such as “the world sends me signs” may train attention to notice and remember matching coincidences while ordinary non-matches fade into the background. Salience, apophenia, and referential thinking are established concepts, but this exact layered architecture and any claim of increased self-relevance weighting in narcissistic organization are **[BRIDGE]/[HYPOTHESIS]**. The pattern is not specific to narcissism and may occur in superstition, spirituality, grief, stress, sleep deprivation, substance use, mania, psychosis, and other contexts. Threat-biased salience may distort current reality; memory reconstruction may later reorganize remembered reality, forming a reinforcing loop. Substances and relationships can modify the runtime in which reality is weighted without changing external reality. Controlled load manipulation may test whether reduced capacity lowers simultaneity and resolution, but such studies require rigorous ethics.
>
> The theory integrates established research traditions, psychodynamic bridges, and original hypotheses. It is not yet a validated clinical theory, diagnostic tool, anatomical model, or deterministic account of parenting. All project-specific mechanisms must remain falsifiable.
